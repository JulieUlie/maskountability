How to use this site

This site publishes specific public claims by companies, with the evidence we reviewed and a verdict. This page explains how to read a record, what happens when a company is reviewed, and what you can do if you have information.

1. Read a record

1

Start with the claim

Each record quotes the exact wording of a public claim and links to the original post. The post is also archived with a timestamp, so the claim can't quietly change.

2

Check the evidence

Evidence is listed one item at a time. Each item says whether it supports the claim, contradicts it, or is neutral, and how it was obtained. The "Limits" line says what the item can't prove.

3

Read the verdict

The verdict describes whether the evidence supports the claim. It does not judge the company's character or intent.

Supported Not supported Insufficient evidence

4

Check the status

Each record shows its status. Only reviewed records are published, and the history shows every change.

2. Search and filter

  • On the list page, type a company name or words from a claim into the search box.
  • Use the verdict menu to show only supported, not supported, or insufficient-evidence records.
  • Press L anywhere outside a text box to switch between light and dark mode. Your choice is remembered in this browser.

3. Check the history

Every record ends with a History list. It shows when the claim was archived, when the verdict was drafted, when a response arrived, and any correction. If a record changes, the change is visible here.

4. If you have information about a claim

Send evidence, not accusations

Useful information includes:

  • A link to the original post, and the date you saw it
  • A screenshot or saved copy, with its date
  • Registry, licence, or official records
  • Reports from a regulator, court, or journalist, with a link

Keep personal details out

Don't send information about private people, such as home addresses, phone numbers, or family details. Records cover companies and public-facing claims only.

For advertising complaints

Misleading advertising, including influencer posts that don't disclose sponsorship, can also be reported to the Jury voor Ethische Praktijken inzake Reclame (JEP) in Belgium, or to the FOD Economie for other issues.

Anyone can send a claim. Use the Send a claim form. Submissions are read by a person and are never published automatically. Records appear only after review. Official bodies for advertising, consumer, registry, and data-protection issues are listed on that page too.

5. If you are a company named on this site

Companies are notified about records through an internal procedure. Responses are handled through that procedure and are not published on this site. When a record is corrected or withdrawn, the change appears in its History list.

Questions

Does a "not supported" verdict mean the company is lying?

No. It means the evidence we reviewed doesn't support the specific claim. Intent isn't assessed, and the record lists what the evidence cannot prove.

Why is a record missing?

Only reviewed records are published. A record may be in review, in its response window, or withdrawn after a correction.

Can I search for a person?

No. Search covers company names and claim text only. Individuals appear only as the author of a specific post, where that matters to a claim.

Is my data stored?

The public site doesn't collect personal data. Nothing is submitted through this page.

Records are versioned. Corrections and withdrawals appear in each record's history.
